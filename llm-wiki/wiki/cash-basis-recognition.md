# Cash-Basis Recognition (canonical rules R1–R11)

> One consistent rule set — THE definition `v_pnl_cash` ([[duckdb-layer]]) implements; fixtures in [[testing-strategy]] derive from these rules. (Revision history in `log.md`.)

> **Close this ruleset before the first posting. (proposed 2026-07-30)** `ruleset_versions {cash_basis: …}` is stamped into every policy set and every retained report header ([[policy-set]]). Revising R1–R11 after books exist restates filed returns silently — the exact failure that page was written to prevent, and `log.md` already records one R1–R9 rewrite.

- **R1 — Recognition events.** A document recognizes on cash basis when it (a) moves a money account (Bank/Cash/Credit Card), (b) charges a credit card — **charge date = payment** (Rev. Rul. 78-38; paying the card bill is a Transfer, no P&L), or (c) records an **owner-paid business expense** — sole prop: the owner's personal payment IS the taxpayer paying (`DR Expense / CR Owner's Contributions`, recognizes at transaction date, [[scenario-catalog]]).
- **R2 — Equity lines never recognize.** Owner's Draw, Owner's Contributions, OBE are equity — excluded from P&L on both bases. A draw is not an expense.
- **R3 — Direct P&L legs on a recognition event** recognize as-is at that document's date — including processing-fee legs on payments, NSF fees, and $0.99-tolerance write-off lines ([[matching-engine]]): they accompany real cash, so cash income ties to bank deposits.
- **R4 — AR/AP settlement.** A recognition event settling a document ([[pairing-and-matching]]) recognizes the settled document's line composition **pro-rata** (applied ÷ document total) at settlement date, across ALL its lines, where: tax lines → liability-collected, never P&L ([[compliance]]); **balance-sheet lines stay balance-sheet** — an asset leg on a bill capitalizes (§263 applies on cash basis too; no laptop in the P&L), and OBE-offset historical documents ([[onboarding-opening-balances]]) recognize nothing — **which is correct only when the prior RETURN was filed on the accrual method. (proposed 2026-07-30)** The composition of a historical open document is chosen at onboarding by `prior_return_basis` ([[onboarding-opening-balances]]): under `ACCRUAL` it offsets OBE and recognizes nothing, because the prior return already reported it; under `CASH` it carries its **real composition** and R4 fires normally at settlement, because the taxpayer has never reported it. The deciding fact is the **filed return, not the bookkeeping** — under the cash method an unpaid invoice is never reported however the books were kept, so "no prior books" can never imply "already reported". Getting this wrong is a *double* omission — income on collection and the deduction on payment — in the migration year, on the first return filed from these books.
- **R5 — Non-cash settlements recognize nothing**: standalone CreditNote, VendorCredit, bad-debt write-off. (Contrast R3: a discount line inside a payment document recognizes; a standalone credit note does not.) **Offset JEs are carved out — see R5a. (proposed 2026-07-30)**
- **R5a — Mutual offsets ARE a recognition event. (proposed 2026-07-30)** A posting that **debits AP carrying a ref and credits AR carrying a ref** (the customer-is-also-a-vendor offset, [[scenario-catalog]]) extinguishes both obligations, which on cash basis is **constructive receipt and constructive payment** on that date. Nothing about the taxpayer's position differs from receiving a cheque and posting one back the same day; R5 previously swept this in with credit notes and was wrong.
  - **The offset amount is `min(Σ AP debits carrying a ref, Σ AR credits carrying a ref)` within the posting.** Each settled document recognizes under R4 pro-rata **on its share of that amount and no more**.
  - **Every other leg of the same posting recognizes nothing** — a bad-debt leg, a forgiveness leg, a discount leg is a non-cash settlement under R5, and **R3 does not fire, because no money account moved**. Without this, a "walk away from the rest" JE fabricates gross receipts and deductions that never happened, and the filed number changes depending on whether the agent wrote one JE or two.
  - If the posting **also** moves a money account, R1(a) already makes it a recognition event and R4/R3 apply unchanged — R5a is needed only for the no-money case.
  - Recognition is identical however the user's story is split across documents. Determinism is the point: the trigger is an amount, not a shape.
- **R6 — Retainers**: recognized at RECEIPT under "Customer deposits received"; the later application is non-cash → no double count.
- **R7 — Refunds / NSF**: negative recognition at their money-moving date.
- **R8 — Transfers**: excluded, both bases.
- **R9 — Sales-tax basis disclosure**: the cash-basis `sales_tax_liability` figure = tax portion of cash received; the report states its basis on its face and warns that many states require accrual-basis remittance regardless of income-tax basis; accrual figure via toggle.
- **R10 — Cutoff: the recognition date is not the clearing date. (proposed 2026-07-30)** Money out by cheque recognizes when the cheque is **written and delivered/mailed**, not when it clears (provided it clears in due course); money in recognizes when the **instrument is received**, not when it is deposited or when the deposit settles. Consequences, because this is where the rule is actually applied: (a) where a statement row *clears an entry already in the books*, the **book date governs** and the clearing only sets `cleared` ([[bank-reconciliation]]); (b) where an import *creates* a draft, the statement date is a **clearing** date — in the first fiscal weeks of a year the agent must ask for the true date rather than silently taking it ([[the-skill]]); (c) the December close carries a cutoff question. A misdated December cheque moves a deduction a whole year and is **invisible to the reconciliation**, which is why the rec alone cannot be the only control.
  - **Cutoff and locking interact, and the interaction must be stated or R10 is inert. (proposed 2026-07-30)** A cheque written 12/28 clears on the *January* statement, imported after the December close already set `locked_through = 12-31`. The correct date is then refused by the lock and redirected into the new year ([[general-ledger-sheet]] rule 4) — the precise error R10 exists to prevent, now paid for with a question. Therefore: **the fiscal-year-end close is not run until the following month's statement has been imported and its pre-year-end items dated** ([[period-locking-month-close]] gains this as a year-end-only precondition), and any cutoff correction found later is an `AMENDMENT` ([[process-instance]]) that unlocks, posts at the true date and re-locks. An `ExceptionKind` cannot carry this — exceptions never block ([[safety-nets]]).
  - **Ask narrowly.** The question is raised only for rows the file itself marks cheque-shaped, within `cutoff_ask_days` of the fiscal year start (an operational threshold on the versioned [[policy-set]], not a phrase). For a **credit-card** import the recognition date *is* the file's transaction date under R1(b) and is never asked. Asking on every early-January row would answer ~18 of 20 questions with data the CSV already contains, and is the interrogation the converting segment bought CSV import to avoid ([[buyer-panel-findings]]).
- **R11 — Cost recovery is basis-independent. (proposed 2026-07-30)** Depreciation, §179, amortization and any other cost-recovery entry recognizes on **both** bases at the entry's own date. The cash method governs the timing of *payments*; it never governs recovery of capitalized cost (§§167/168/179 apply identically to a cash-method filer). Mechanically: **a P&L line whose contra is a contra-asset, accumulated-amortization or prepaid-asset account is a recognition event on both bases.** Without R11, R1–R10 is an allowlist that no depreciation JE can match, so `v_pnl_cash` reports Schedule C line 13 as **$0 forever** — while [[v1-scope]] explicitly requires depreciation as a manual JE. R4's balance-sheet carve-out routes the cost *to* the balance sheet; R11 is what brings it back. Without both, a timing rule silently becomes a permanent disallowance.

CPA endorsements on record (Dan review): card-charge-date, retainers-at-receipt, pro-rata incl. tax line, bad-debt-nothing, NSF-at-reversal. Accrual vs cash on any fixture ledger must differ *exactly* by unpaid/non-cash items ([[testing-strategy]] layer-5 gate).
